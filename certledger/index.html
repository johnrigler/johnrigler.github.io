CertLedger

This paper has a public fingerprint.

A CertLedger label connects a physical document to the SHA-256 fingerprint of the exact PDF that was finalized before printing and to a public Litecoin ledger record containing that fingerprint.

You scanned a CertLedger label

The QR code on the paper is meant to answer one narrow question: can this exact document be identified later?

CertLedger does not decide whether the statements in a document are true. It preserves a verifiable relationship between a document, its cryptographic fingerprint, and a public record that can be examined independently.

The bridge from paper to ledger

final browser document ↓ canonical PDF ↓ SHA-256 fingerprint ↓ Litecoin transaction / OP_RETURN ↓ CertLedger verification record ↓ one-inch QR label on paper

The label is applied after the canonical PDF is frozen and hashed. The paper label therefore points back to the digital artifact rather than becoming part of the artifact it identifies.

Why the timestamp matters

Once the document fingerprint is included in a public Litecoin transaction, the record becomes globally observable through independent ledger infrastructure. Anyone can later compare the PDF's SHA-256 value with the value referenced by that transaction.

The blockchain record is not a substitute for certified mail, testimony, or ordinary legal evidence. It adds a separate, public chronology and integrity check to those conventional processes.

Verify a PDF yourself

If you have the electronic PDF, calculate its SHA-256 digest locally and compare it with the digest shown in its CertLedger record.

sha256sum document.pdf

Other operating systems and tools can calculate SHA-256 as well. The verification does not require trusting this website.
